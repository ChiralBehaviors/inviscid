Two Fields, A Million Cells

The soliton is a plane wave: every cell in a plane across the medium does the same thing, and what travels is a slab. So it belongs in bulk, and here it is in bulk — a block of 1,036,800 cells with the packet running through it.

But the interesting number is the other one. What this page computes is 256 numbers: one fold angle and one displacement for each of 128 planes. Everything else is drawing. That is not a shortcut or a level of detail — it is exact, and it is gated: from any state where the cells are unrotated and displaced along the axis, the full seven-coordinate force has no torque and no transverse component, to 10⁻¹⁶, so the coordinates the reduction drops can never be excited. A million cells of this medium, doing this, are two hundred and fifty-six numbers.

That gap is the whole point, and it is the third time this project has used it. It is why two million cells integrate in a fragment shader, and it is what lets a simulation be larger than anything we could draw cell by cell.

what each rung costs, and what gates it

The hierarchy

Nothing here is a coarse-graining or an approximation with an error budget. Each rung is an exact statement about the medium that some measurement pins to machine precision, and each one is why a particular page in this project can exist at the size it does.

Rung 2 is the one that bought the most. Once the fold law is known to be exactly 8γ̈ = −k(12γ − 2Σγ) with integer coefficients, the simulation stops being a constrained multibody problem and becomes a scalar wave on a graph — which is why the live page integrates two million cells in a fragment shader at 120 frames a second. Rung 3 is the one this page uses: the wave is a plane wave, so the block's million cells are two arrays of 128.

Rung 4 is the honest gap. The soliton's carrier is four cells and its envelope is tens to hundreds, and those two scales separate cleanly — which is exactly the condition for evolving the envelope on its own coarse grid and dropping the carrier entirely. It would buy another order of magnitude and it is the level at which the description becomes genuinely scale-free. It is not built, and this page does not pretend otherwise.

how far the scaling goes, and what stops it at each end

Scale-free, between two walls

The solitons are a one-parameter family with A · w = 48: double the amplitude and halve the width and you have another one. That inverse law is a scaling symmetry — the same wave at different sizes — and it is the sense in which this medium is scale-free.

How far does the scaling actually go? Judge every member on the same dimensionless clock — let each travel twenty-six of its own envelope widths — and the answer is measured, not assumed.

Below, the lattice bites. When the envelope is only a few cells across, the packet can no longer ignore that space is discrete: at w = 4 it breathes by a quarter of its height, at w = 3 more, and by w = 2 it has lost a quarter of its height outright. That wall is the lattice constant, and it is not adjustable.

Above, there is no wall — not along this axis, and that corrected a guess. The fold and strain branches phase-match as the carrier gets long, so a broad carrier is fatal; a broad envelope at a fixed carrier is not, because widening the envelope detunes nothing. The family holds to w = 32 and an amplitude of 1.5° with no measurable loss — a fivefold range of scale above the discreteness wall, tested. The second wall is real but it lives on the other axis — the carrier wavenumber, not the envelope width — and that is where the broad pulse on the soliton page dies: half its height gone by t = 200, into the strain wave it drives.

honesty

What this page is not

The million cells are a rendering of a one-dimensional field, not a million independent degrees of freedom. Saying otherwise would be the exact overclaim the gates exist to prevent. What is true, and is the point, is that for this class of motion the two descriptions are the same — not approximately, exactly — so the drawing costs a million and the physics costs seven hundred.

This is a plane wave, not a three-dimensionally localized soliton. Whether the medium carries a fully localized packet — one confined across the beam as well as along it — is a different question and an open one. Focusing media generally make such things unstable, and nothing here has been measured about it.

The integrator in this page is checked, not trusted. At load it runs the same two-field chain on a small ring against reference frames from the gated Python model, and badges the worst deviation; it also re-runs the check with one coupling deliberately wrong, to show the check can fail. Both numbers are in the readout above.
