what makes this honest

The page is the integrator, and it is checked

The kick established, at machine precision, that the medium's fold sector is an exactly linear scalar field with an integer-exact operator — mass 8, onsite 12k, six couplings of −2k, every missing boundary bond tied to the twin sheet (gates R5, R5b, R9). Given that, the entire simulation is the law above, and a GPU can integrate two million cells of it faster than real time. This page does exactly that: float32, velocity Verlet, dt = 0.01 — the same scheme, step and constants as the model's own verlet_fold.

The self-check. At load, the page runs its own shader pipeline on a double(8) block — same code path, small textures — for the full 24,000 steps, and compares 31 sampled frames against reference frames exported from the gated Python integrator (export-ref, shipped inside this page). The badge above reports the measured maximum deviation, as a fraction of the reference peak: float32 against the model's float64. If that number is ever large, do not trust the pictures.

Scope. Same as the whole kick lane: harmonic, linearised, at a = −30°, k = 1. The display normalises color to the running field maximum, so late, faint ringing stays visible; the readout gives the true scale. What a bigger side buys is extent, not mesh: the lattice constant is the medium — here the fronts get 50 cells of clean flight before the twin-sheet crossing, against 14 on the recorded pages.

Things to try. Kick two cells far apart and watch the fringe system build where the cones cross. Kick a line of cells to make a plane wave. Kick opposite signs close together — a dipole — and watch the lobes. The sound cones are the exact prediction, radius ½ per unit time; everything else is the law, live.
