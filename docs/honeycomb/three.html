inviscid · three cells of the honeycomb

The triangles hold; the squares let go

Two cuboctahedron cells with an octahedral hole cell between them. Through the whole exchange each triangular contact keeps all three of its shared vertices — that is what holds the medium together. The square contact between the two VEs does not: it starts with four shared vertices, drops to two, and ends on one.

The reciprocal condition holds. The earlier two-cell result only imposed one VE's eight faces against its eight holes; it never checked that a hole's other faces meet their own VEs. They do — hole ∩ VE₂ stays at three shared vertices, exact to 6e-16, at every phase from 0 to −60°. So the chain closes in both directions and the exchange is consistent along it.

Two kinds of contact

The honeycomb's faces are not equivalent. Triangular faces are rigid — they belong to two cells at once and stay whole. Square faces are the folding squares of the jitterbug: they open as the motion runs, so the contact across them decays.

The VE-to-VE square contact loses vertices as the square folds: four at a = 0, two through the middle of the exchange, one at a = −60° where the cell has become an octahedron and only a corner is left.

What carries the coupling

If anything transmits through this medium it goes by the triangular faces. They are the only contact that survives the motion intact, and each is a whole rigid triangle shared by exactly two cells.

Scope. Three cells of the periodic structure with no rotation on any cell (verified zero) and λ taken from the fold half-diagonal. Shared-vertex counts are exact coincidences at 1e-8. This shows the chain is consistent; it is still not a mobility computation for the whole honeycomb.
