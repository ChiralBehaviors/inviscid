Eight around one, and the whole cluster turns inside out

One octahedral cell with its full complement of eight cuboctahedra, every contact a shared triangular face. Run the exchange to a = −60° and the roles invert completely: the centre opens into a cuboctahedron while all eight neighbours close into octahedra. Every one of the eight faces stays glued to 8e-16 the entire way.

At a = 0 this is Fuller's packing: an octahedron of radius 1 wrapped by eight cuboctahedra of radius √2. Drive the phase down and every shared face holds — the worst vertex mismatch across all eight is 8.3e-16 at a = −60°, where the centre has become the cuboctahedron (radius √2) and each of the eight has become an octahedron (radius 1). The cluster is the same object at both ends, with the roles exchanged. Use the shell slider to fade the outer cells and watch the centre open.

One degree of freedom

The whole cluster runs on a single parameter. The centre's phase is a + 60 exactly, the lattice is the fold half-diagonal, and no cell rotates. A scan over hole phases finds no other closure — this motion, or nothing.

The squares still let go

The twelve VE-to-VE square contacts around the cluster decay together, 4 → 2 → 1 shared vertices, while all eight triangular faces stay whole. The two kinds of face are not equivalent, and only one is structural.

Widest in the middle

λ rises from 1 to 2/√3 at a = −30°, where centre and shell are congruent, then returns to 1. The cluster breathes open to let the cells trade places and closes again.

Scope. Nine cells of the periodic honeycomb with zero rotation on every cell and λ from the fold half-diagonal. It shows that the local cluster is consistent through the full exchange; it is still not a mobility computation, so whether the honeycomb has motions beyond this single collective one is open — and that is the question that decides whether it can carry a wave.
