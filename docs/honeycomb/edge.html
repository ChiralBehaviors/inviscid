inviscid · two adjacent vector equilibria

Four struts meet, and nothing holds them

Neighbouring VEs in this packing touch at a single vertex — but all four struts arriving there from one unit run exactly collinear with four from the other, dot product −1.000000000, at every phase. The coupling is four straight lines through a point, not a socket. And no constraint in the model resists pulling them apart.

Hold pull apart at zero and sweep the phase: the units open together, the vertex gap stays at machine zero, and the four strut pairs stay collinear to nine decimal places the whole way. That is a real invariant of the motion — each strut of one unit continues as a strut of its neighbour, in a straight line, at every phase. Then move pull apart. Nothing resists. The contact opens, no plate is ever interpenetrated, and no constraint anywhere in the model objects.

Why this is not a ball joint

A ball joint asserts the two vertices stay coincident — it resists separation. Nothing here does. Under thin, rigid, impenetrable plates the only interaction is non-interpenetration, which is one-sided: it stops the units passing through each other and says nothing about them drifting apart.

What I got wrong

I restored exactly this constraint to the solver, having shown its label was wrong — three rows is position-only, not a rigid pin. But a wrong label is not a wrong omission. Leaving it out may have been the correct modelling call, badly documented.

What still needs a mechanism

Collinearity is a striking invariant, but an invariant is not a force. Something has to carry tension across that point, or the array is a packing that falls apart the moment it is driven — which is precisely what it did.

Scope. Both units here are held on the symmetric path, so this shows the geometry of the coupling, not the behaviour of a driven array. Everything drawn is computed from the closed form p(a) = √2·cos a·m + √(2/3)·sin a·b, which reproduces the real vertices to 9e-13.
